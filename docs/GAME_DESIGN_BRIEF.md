# Anomaly Detector: Design Brief

Status: agreed MVP direction. `Anomaly Detector` is a working title, not a cleared brand.

## Product

`Anomaly Detector` is a synchronous, competitive, browser-based deduction strategy game for 2-4 players. Research teams compete aboard an orbital station that studies an unknown natural anomaly. A corporation will award a single funding tender to the team with the highest final rating.

The game borrows only the genre-level premise of deduction from incomplete experimental evidence. It has an original setting, terminology, directed experiments, tender structure, action flow, visual expression, and rules. Do not copy any text, art, terminology, components, scoring tables, formulas, turn structure, or app behaviour from *Alchemists*.

MVP target: a standard match with 2-4 real players, about 45 minutes for four players. The first release is a mobile-first responsive web app in portrait orientation, with a full desktop layout.

## Core Fantasy

Players are competing R&D contractors. They capture telemetry from six named anomaly signals, run directed laboratory experiments, build a model of new physics, and turn verified effects into exclusive corporate contracts for energy control and deep-space communications.

The anomaly is natural, not an ancient artefact or a hidden code. The tone is grounded corporate sci-fi, without parody or a cartoonishly evil corporation.

## Victory

Five fixed rounds end with a final corporate audit. The team with the highest public final rating wins the tender.

- About 60% of rating comes from commercial contracts.
- About 40% comes from science: confirmed theses and the final fundamental model.
- The final model gives partial credit for each correct signal property and a substantial bonus for a complete correct model.
- Tie-breakers, in order: higher research confirmation, more remaining budget, then shared victory.
- The score breakdown is public after every round. The unsubmitted portion of a team's final model stays hidden until the audit, with its possible score range shown.

Future rating categories are reserved in the data model but not implemented: patents, station infrastructure, station safety, and long-term research programmes.

## Shared Hidden World

Each match has a server-generated, reproducible, solvable anomaly configuration. The server stores the seed.

- The registry always shows six stable signal names: `Aster`, `Boreal`, `Cinder`, `Delta`, `Eclipse`, and `Ferro`.
- Each signal has two hidden properties: a field type and a polarity.
- Players see four public result classes from a test: transmission gain, attenuation, reflection, and unstable collapse.
- Results are generated by directed interactions, not unordered combinations. A test chooses a source signal, a different receiver signal, and an emission protocol.
- A signal sample does not get consumed. Scarcity comes from access to new telemetry, laboratory capacity, budget, and timing.
- A newly discovered signal is public knowledge, but its raw telemetry remains private to the discovering team until laboratory use.

At match start each team receives one private signal sample, two budget, and one basic analytical report. Two signals are publicly available from round one.

## Laboratory And Deduction

The starting protocols are:

- Impulse: fast and inexpensive; gives a coarse public result class.
- Continuous: costs more and, with enough power, gives the initiator an extra private measurement such as flow direction or stability range.

Signals cannot be tested against themselves in the MVP. Self-tests may become a future technology.

Every team has a private interactive deduction model. It supports possible-property marks, exclusions, personal hypotheses, and optional notes. It warns only about contradictions with confirmed public facts; it never solves the puzzle or recommends the next test.

Teams may use the `model analysis` action to submit one public, immediately checked thesis. A correct thesis improves scientific rating and creates a first-discoverer advantage. A wrong thesis does not erase earned rating, but temporarily blocks one unit of contract power until the team completes a successful laboratory test.

Private final-model drafts can change freely until the final audit.

## Round Structure

Each match has five rounds.

1. Teams secretly choose an access slot.
2. The server resolves slots and reveals the round order.
3. Teams openly declare their power allocation in reverse slot order, from latest access to earliest access.
4. Operations resolve by phase, using the access order: reconnaissance, laboratory tests, model analysis, then contracts.
5. The end-of-round calculation applies grants, contracts, trust, rating, and new available work.

The first-position tie priority rotates once per round. It resolves otherwise equal slot conflicts and is public before choices begin.

### Access Slots

There are always six fixed slots, even with fewer than four teams. Each team secretly chooses one. If several teams select the same slot, the conflict winner keeps it; the others move to the nearest available later slot, one at a time by the rotating tie priority.

| Slot | Access order | Cost | Compensation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Emergency | 1 | 2 budget | None |
| Priority | 2 | 1 budget | None |
| Standard | 3 | None | None |
| Off-peak | 4 | None | 1 signal sample |
| Night | 5 | None | 1 analytical report |
| Remote | 6 | None | 1 budget and 1 signal sample |

Slot compensation is fixed in the MVP. Early access grants operational priority; late access grants planning information and resources.

### Power

Each team has four power units per round, allocated openly among four action categories. No category may receive more than two units.

| Category | One power | Two power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reconnaissance | Acquire one sample | Acquire two samples or access the unknown high-value zone |
| Laboratory | One impulse test | One continuous, precise test |
| Model analysis | Submit one thesis | Submit a thesis with extended verification |
| Contract | Submit one bid | Improve that bid's price or evidence |

Power allocation fixes only the category and amount. A team picks its exact target when its turn arrives in that phase. It can revise any choice before confirmation; confirmed actions are final. The unknown reconnaissance zone gives a more valuable but uncertain result, while other zones display their expected kind of finding.

## Contracts, Budget, And Trust

Each round exposes `number of players + 1` exclusive normal contracts. Every contract has two possible fulfilment paths. A team can win at most one normal contract per round.

- When a team reaches the contract phase, it publicly reserves its chosen contract; only the chosen contract is visible, not bid quality.
- A second team may intentionally challenge a reserved contract.
- A bid is ranked by fit to the requested public effect, evidence level, and requested funding. The current round's access order breaks a complete tie.
- Unclaimed or failed contracts expire at round end and are replaced.
- One valuable final contract is visible from round one and resolves in round five. It is worth about 15-20% of the maximum rating, never enough to invalidate an otherwise better full match.

Contracts require both an appropriate verified effect and a minimum evidence level. A more accurate model can improve the bid's cost and strength.

The budget economy is a fixed operational grant each round, contract payouts, and late-slot compensation. Corporate trust gates access to high-value contracts; it is not a score multiplier. There are no permanent laboratory upgrades in the MVP. Contract rewards may grant one-round benefits only.

## Timing And Failure Handling

For a four-player game:

- 45 seconds: secret access-slot choice.
- 60 seconds: power allocation.
- Up to 90 seconds per operational phase, with up to 20 seconds per team's choice and unused time passing to the next team.

If a player times out or disconnects, the server takes conservative, non-beneficial defaults: a standard or later free slot without budget spend, unallocated power to reserve, and a skipped unresolved target. The player may reconnect and continue. A missing player never blocks a match.

## Modes And Match Setup

- Standard rooms are private, invite-code rooms for exactly 2, 3, or 4 real players.
- The host chooses the room size. A game cannot start until every chosen slot is occupied, and the host must explicitly confirm start.
- There are no bots, public matchmaking, global ranks, chat, monetisation, purchases, random crisis events, or permanent upgrades in the MVP.
- A short solo tutorial teaches one guided round.
- A non-ranked learning room for 2-4 friends uses four signals and three rounds, without trust penalties or the final contract.

## Audit, History, And Privacy

After every finished game, participants receive a complete post-match audit and a read-only phase-by-phase replay. It reveals the anomaly configuration, all private measurements, bids, actions, seed-derived results, score sources, and tie-break resolution.

Only match participants can access its audit and replay. Public sharing links are not supported.

Accounts have match history. On account deletion, remove the profile and identity-provider links while retaining finished match records in anonymised form as `Deleted participant`, so other participants keep a coherent audit.

## Accounts, Data, And Russian Launch Scope

The initial audience is Russian citizens. The product operator is a Russian citizen acting as an individual. The application therefore must be designed as an operator of personal data, not as a private family-use tool.

- Primary sign-in: Yandex ID and VK ID.
- Do not use foreign email as a Russian-user registration mechanism.
- Do not treat Telegram OAuth as a compliant primary login method without a separate legal review.
- Do not store passwords. Email magic links are out of the MVP.
- Store Russian users' personal data, identity links, and match history in Russia. The production target is Yandex Cloud through `yc`.
- Publish an accessible personal-data processing policy before collecting user data. Before launch, obtain legal review for the Roskomnadzor notification obligation, authentication providers, consent flows, and retention policy.
- Product age marking for the MVP: `16+`.

This is a product requirement, not legal advice. Revalidate all legal and provider requirements immediately before launch.

## Language And Interface

- Base locale and fallback: `ru`.
- The first release defaults to Russian. Locale is a manual profile setting and is retained for later translations.
- All user-facing copy is externalised by domain-specific i18n chunks, including auth, lobby, match, tutorial, audit, validation errors, contracts, and legal documents. Do not hard-code player-visible text in UI components.
- Mobile portrait is a first-class interface. The deduction model opens as a dedicated full-screen view; the match timer and current phase remain continuously visible.
- Desktop is a full product surface, using a multi-panel workspace for the model, experiment log, contracts, and active phase.
- Meaning is never conveyed by colour alone. Test results and signal properties use a colour, a distinct icon, and short text.

## Technical Product Constraints

- The backend is authoritative for the hidden configuration, seeds, timers, validation, results, scores, contracts, and audit. Browser clients receive only public and player-authorised private information.
- Real-time state must support reconnecting players and a deterministic replay log.
- Use the existing `webapp` and `backend/API` surfaces. The public `website` and native mobile app remain deferred.
- Deployment is not part of the current implementation scope. When it begins, use Yandex Cloud through the `yc` CLI and follow `docs/YANDEX_CLOUD.md`.

## Explicitly Deferred

- More than two active victory categories.
- Public matchmaking, global ratings, seasons, anti-fraud ranking systems, and spectating.
- Chat and shared replay links.
- Native applications, PWA/offline support, and public marketing site work.
- Payments and monetisation.
- Bots for standard matches, random events, self-tests, permanent upgrades, and variable slot compensation.
- International legal and identity-provider support beyond the Russian launch scope.

## Validation Before Feature Expansion

Before expanding the MVP, prototype and playtest the core loop with the following questions:

1. Can a new player understand a directed test and use the deduction model after the tutorial?
2. Does early access versus late compensation create meaningful but fair choices?
3. Can commercial and scientific paths both win across 2, 3, and 4 players?
4. Are contract challenges rare, intentional, and understandable?
5. Does a four-player standard game complete within 40-50 minutes?
6. Can every final result be explained unambiguously by the audit and replay?
